# How Deep Should Garage Shelves Be? Worked Examples for UK Garages

The right garage shelf depth depends less on what is being stored and more on **how much width is left once the car is parked**. In a UK single garage that is often very little.

## The calculation

```
space for side shelving = garage width − car width (with mirrors) − driver-door space − passenger-side gap
```

UK cars are right-hand drive, so the driver door opens towards the right-hand wall (looking in from the garage door). Shelving normally goes on the **passenger side** or the **back wall**.

## Worked examples: 3m wide garage

Assumptions: 0.6m to open the driver door and step out; 0.1m clearance on the passenger side.

| Car (width incl. mirrors) | Space left | Side shelving that fits |
|---|---|---|
| Ford Puma (1.93m) | 0.37m | 300mm deep |
| Kia Sportage (2.08m) | 0.22m | none |
| Tesla Model Y (2.13m) | 0.17m | none |

Car widths: [Direct Line Group, 2024](https://www.directlinegroup.co.uk/en/news/brand-news/2024/20112024.html).

In a 2.4m wide older garage, none of these cars leaves room for side shelving at all.

## The back wall is usually the answer

A 6m long garage holding a 4.5m car still has roughly 0.9m behind it after keeping 30cm clear of the door frame and 30cm behind the bumper. That is enough for **600mm deep shelving**, which takes a standard 64L storage box lengthways.

## Depth guide

| Depth | Best for |
|---|---|
| 300mm | Beside a parked car; paint tins, tools, small boxes |
| 450mm | Narrow garages with a small car |
| 600mm | Back wall or storage-only garages; full-size storage boxes |
